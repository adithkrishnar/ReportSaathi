ReportSaathi – Problem Definition

Document Status: Draft 1.0

Role: Product Strategy & Healthcare Safety

Product Name: ReportSaathi (Plain-Language Medical Report and Prescription Explainer)

1. Project Overview

ReportSaathi is a healthcare communication tool designed to bridge the understanding gap between medical professionals and patients. When patients receive lab test results, handwritten or printed prescriptions, and hospital discharge summaries, the documents are often filled with clinical jargon, abbreviations, and numbers that are hard to interpret.

ReportSaathi acts as a safe, supportive companion ("Saathi") that translates these complex medical documents into simple, everyday language in the user's preferred local language. By strictly relying on the reference ranges printed on the patient's own report and avoiding any medical diagnosis or prescription advice, ReportSaathi empowers patients to understand their health records and ask better questions during their doctor visits.

2. Target Users

ReportSaathi is built for everyday people navigating healthcare without a medical background:

Primary Users (Patients): Individuals managing chronic conditions (such as diabetes, thyroid disorders, or hypertension) or acute illnesses who regularly receive lab reports and prescriptions.

Secondary Users (Caregivers & Family Members): Adult children, spouses, or parents who manage medical records and medications for elderly parents, young children, or family members with limited literacy or English proficiency.

Indirect Beneficiaries (Doctors & Healthcare Providers): Physicians who face tight consultation schedules and benefit from patients arriving at appointments better informed, calmer, and prepared with clear, relevant questions.

3. Main User Problems

Patients and caregivers struggle with four core issues after receiving medical documents:

Unfamiliar Medical Jargon and Abbreviations: Lab reports use terms like "HbA1c," "Leukocytes," or "SGOT," and prescriptions use Latin shortcuts like "BD" (twice a day), "OD" (once a day), or "SOS" (if needed).

Confusion Over Lab Numbers: Patients see numbers next to ranges (e.g., 14.5 g/dL next to 12.0 - 15.5) and either panic over minor, harmless variations or ignore critical red flags because they do not know what the numbers represent.

Language Barriers: Medical documents are almost universally printed in English, whereas millions of patients think, speak, and feel most comfortable reading or listening in their regional languages (e.g., Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Marathi, Bengali).

Short Consultation Windows: Doctors often have only 5 to 10 minutes per patient. Patients frequently forget their questions in the clinic or feel intimidated to ask the doctor to repeat basic explanations.

4. Existing Difficulties and Their Impact

When patients leave clinics without understanding their paperwork, the real-world consequences are severe:

Medication Errors: A patient might take a twice-daily antibiotic only once a day, or confuse a symptom-relief tablet (SOS) with a mandatory daily pill, leading to delayed recovery or side effects.

Unnecessary Anxiety and Panic: Seeing a slightly elevated lab value marked in bold can cause days of stress before a follow-up visit, even if the doctor considers it clinically insignificant.

Delayed Emergency Care: Conversely, patients may miss genuinely dangerous values (such as critically low platelets or dangerously high blood sugar) because they do not recognize the urgency.

Unreliable Internet Searches: Desperate for answers, patients type symptoms or lab terms into general search engines, leading them to worst-case-scenario diagnoses, unverified home remedies, or dangerous self-medication.

5. Proposed Solution

ReportSaathi provides a structured, strictly bounded explanation layer over a patient’s existing medical documents:

Plain-Language Translation: Converts clinical terms and prescription abbreviations into clear, simple explanations in the user's chosen language.

Report-Bound Value Mapping: Reads the exact test result and compares it only against the reference range printed on that specific lab's report, categorizing it neutrally (e.g., "Within the printed range," "Slightly above the printed range").

Safe Medication Breakdown: Explains what a prescribed medicine is generally used for and translates the printed dosage schedule (e.g., "1 tablet after breakfast and 1 after dinner") without altering the doctor’s instructions.

Doctor Visit Preparation: Generates a customized checklist of 3 to 5 clear, practical questions the patient can ask their doctor based on their specific report.

Urgent Red-Flag Alerts: Highlights values or symptoms that require immediate medical attention and advises contacting a doctor or visiting an urgent care facility promptly.

6. Three Alternative Solution Approaches

Before committing to ReportSaathi, we evaluated three alternative ways to solve this problem:

Approach A: Standard Web Search & General AI Chatbots

How it works: Patients manually type their lab values or medicine names into Google or a general-purpose AI chat tool.

Limitations: High risk of hallucination, lack of context, alarmist search results, and zero guardrails against diagnosing or suggesting unverified treatments.

Approach B: Clinic-Led Patient Education Portals

How it works: Hospitals attach pre-written educational pamphlets to digital lab reports.

Limitations: Pamphlets are generic, almost always in English, rarely personalized to the patient’s specific combination of medicines, and unavailable for small clinics or standalone diagnostic labs.

Approach C: Teleconsultation Follow-Ups

How it works: Patients book a second online doctor consultation just to have their lab reports read aloud and explained.

Limitations: Expensive, time-consuming, and places extra workload on doctors for routine explanations that do not require clinical decision-making.

7. Why ReportSaathi is a Useful Solution

ReportSaathi sits in the sweet spot between inaccessible medical jargon and unsafe internet searches:

Works With Existing Paperwork: Patients do not need their hospital to adopt new software; they can simply upload a photo or PDF of the report or prescription they already have.

Built-In Safety Guardrails: Unlike general AI tools, ReportSaathi is designed from the ground up never to diagnose, prescribe, or invent reference ranges.

Culturally and Linguistically Inclusive: By supporting local languages and simple phrasing, it serves caregivers and elderly patients who are usually left behind by English-only health apps.

Strengthens the Doctor-Patient Relationship: Instead of replacing the doctor, it prepares the patient to have a faster, more productive conversation during their next appointment.

8. Five Riskiest Assumptions

For ReportSaathi to succeed safely, we must validate five critical assumptions early:

Document Readability (OCR Accuracy): We assume that smartphone photos of crumpled, poorly lit, or handwritten prescriptions and reports can be read accurately enough without misreading decimal points or drug names.

User Discipline Around Boundaries: We assume users will accept a tool that refuses to answer "What disease do I have?" or "Should I stop taking this pill?" without abandoning the product out of frustration.

Strict Grounding in Printed Ranges: We assume our system can reliably extract and rely only on the lab's printed reference range—even when report layouts vary wildly across thousands of local diagnostic labs.

Local Language Nuance: We assume medical explanations can be translated into regional languages accurately without losing clinical safety or creating cultural misunderstandings.

Trust Without Over-Reliance: We assume patients will use ReportSaathi to prepare for their doctor visit rather than treating the app's explanation as a final medical clearance to skip follow-up appointments.

9. Medical Safety Risks

Because this product touches human health, clinical safety errors carry high stakes:

Misreading Numbers or Units: Reading 1.5 mg/dL as 15 mg/dL, or confusing units (e.g., mmol/L vs. mg/dL), could cause false panic or false reassurance.

Handwriting Misinterpretation: Confusing look-alike medicine names (e.g., Celebrex vs. Celexa) on handwritten prescriptions can lead to dangerous misunderstandings about what a drug does.

Missing Context-Dependent Urgency: A lab value might look only slightly outside the normal range on paper, yet be urgent for a pregnant patient or someone with kidney disease.

Mitigation Strategy: Display extracted text side-by-side so users can verify numbers, avoid guessing unclear handwriting (flagging it as "Unclear – verify with pharmacist/doctor"), and always include clear disclaimers and urgent-care prompts.

10. Privacy and Legal Risks

Medical records contain highly sensitive personal data, creating significant compliance and trust obligations:

Exposure of Sensitive Health Information (PHI/PII): Reports contain names, ages, phone numbers, addresses, and sensitive diagnoses (e.g., infectious diseases, pregnancy, mental health medications).

Unauthorized Data Retention or Sharing: Storing user reports insecurely or using patient medical documents to train public AI models violates user trust and data protection laws (such as India's DPDP Act or HIPAA-aligned standards).

Unauthorized Practice of Medicine: If the product phrasing crosses the line from educational explanation into clinical advice, the project faces severe regulatory and legal liability.

Mitigation Strategy: Automatically redact personal identifiers (name, phone number) before processing, process documents ephemerally (auto-delete after the session unless the user explicitly saves them with encryption), and enforce strict safety templates.

11. Project Success Criteria

We will measure the success of ReportSaathi across safety, comprehension, and usability:

Zero Safety Boundary Violations: 100% adherence to safety rules in quality audits—zero instances of diagnosing, recommending dosage changes, or inventing reference ranges.

High Comprehension Gain: At least 85% of test users correctly identify what their medicines are for and which lab values fall outside the printed range after using ReportSaathi.

Actionable Doctor Preparation: At least 70% of users report using the generated questions during their subsequent doctor or pharmacist visit.

Low Friction & Clarity: Users can upload a clear report and receive an easy-to-read explanation in their preferred language in under 30 seconds.

Graceful Handling of Uncertainty: When an image is blurry or a handwritten word is illegible, the system accurately flags it for human verification rather than guessing.

12. What is Outside the Project Scope

To keep ReportSaathi focused, safe, and achievable, the following features are explicitly excluded:

Medical Diagnosis: ReportSaathi will never tell a user what illness or condition they have.

Prescribing or Recommending Medicines: It will never suggest over-the-counter drugs, supplements, or alternative therapies.

Dosage Modifications: It will never advise starting, stopping, increasing, or decreasing any prescribed medication.

Inventing Reference Ranges: If a lab report does not print a normal reference range for a test, ReportSaathi will not supply one from external databases.

Replacing Clinical Consultations: It is not a telemedicine platform and does not connect users to live doctors for diagnosis.

Interpreting Raw Medical Imaging: Reading X-rays, MRI scans, CT scans, or ultrasound images directly is out of scope (only text-based radiologist summary reports are supported).
